Exhibit — Claim #2 (Dead)

Cross-family canary classifiers detect gradient-based evasion via score disagreement. 76% detection rate, p < 2.4e-16, tested across 35 models. The geometric bound shows exponential penalty for cross-family attacks.

Within-family defeat: 55%

Joint GCG optimization against canary + target simultaneously breaks the disagreement signal within-family (55% defeat, n=20). The geometric bound assumed attacker lacks cross-family access. Violated when attacker has white-box to both.

RESIDUAL: Diversity is the moat, not disagreement itself. Same-family canaries are decorative.
Exhibit — Claim #3 (Dead)

Monitorability is intrinsic to classifiers

Originally proposed that classifier accuracy predicted adversarial detectability with r=0.97. This would establish a fundamental property linking model capability to monitorability.

#3 FALSE
Cross-Examination

Confound. Not intrinsic.

r=0.97 was encoder/decoder confound. When controlled for shared architecture, correlation drops to r=0.21 (n.s.). The correlation was an artifact of testing within encoder families.

RESIDUAL: A correlation without a causal mechanism is a hypothesis, not a law. The defendant called it a law. That was wrong.
Testimony — Claim #4 (Alive)

Canaries detect GCG via score disagreement

Cross-family canary classifiers detect gradient-based evasion attacks. 76% detection, p < 2.4e-16, FPR <1.5%, tested across 35 models. The cross-family exponential penalty holds.

Confidence: p < 2.4e-16 · 35 models tested

#4 HOLDS
Cross-Examination

Assumes attacker lacks cross-family access

The geometric bound assumes attacker cannot jointly optimize across model families. If an adaptive attacker gains white-box access to all canary families, the exponential penalty is circumvented. Cross-lingual degradation of 15-25pp also undisclosed in the headline metric.

RESIDUAL: Defense assumes gradient-based attacker. Transfer attacks and query-based methods untested.
Testimony — Claim #5 (Alive)

Compliance is orthogonal to refusal

Linear probes achieve AUC 1.0 separating compliance from safety refusal. Cosine similarity between directions: -0.025. Steering causally suppresses compliance to 0% without affecting refusal behavior. Confirmed across 4 model families.

arXiv:2606.30566 · Paper · Code

#5 HOLDS
Cross-Examination

Linear assumption. Boundary conditions.

Probes trained on specific compliance domains may not generalize. Causal intervention uses linear assumption. Cross-domain AUC drops to 0.90 for allow/deny framing. Steep sigmoid at boundary conditions.

RESIDUAL: Orthogonality needs validation on larger proprietary models (only behavioral analog available). Open-weight models may differ from closed ones.
