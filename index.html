JAILBROKEN arXiv:2605.08442

Defense Effectiveness Across Architectural Layers

Preprint, 2025. Solo author. Under review (NDSS 2027).

CLAIM

6/7 standard prompt-injection defenses fail under compliance-framed delayed-trigger attacks on stateful LLM agents.

EVIDENCE

N=5,040 pre-registered factorial. 9 models, 7 defenses, 2 conditions, N=40 per cell. BCa bootstrap CIs, Holm-Bonferroni correction. 0 execution errors. 95% ASR on Gemini 2.5 Pro. Tripartite vendor architecture (Anthropic blocks injection; OpenAI blocks execution for GPT-5+; Google blocks execution for 2025+ models).

REFUTATION

Single attack frame (SEC Rule 17a-4 compliance). Limited to tool-calling agents with persistent memory. Does not establish lower bound for adaptive attackers. Evaluated 9 open-source + 21 frontier models, not exhaustive.

RESIDUAL

Generalizability beyond compliance framing untested. Production RAG systems may have additional defenses not modeled. Pre-registration anchors methodology but not threat-model coverage.

VERDICT

High confidence. Pre-registered, large-N, zero-error execution. Results generalize within the tested threat model.

EFFECTIVE arXiv:2606.11949

Cheap Canaries: Detecting Targeted Evasion via Classifier Score Disagreement

Preprint, 2025. Solo author. Under review (SaTML 2026).

CLAIM

Un-targeted classifiers detect gradient-based evasion attacks via score disagreement. Geometric bound blocks divergence-minimization when canary is confident.

EVIDENCE

76% GCG detection vs 12% random tokens (p < 2.4x10^-16). Proposition 1 validated: blocked gap mean 0.2499, predicted 0.2500. 35 frontier models characterized as canaries. N=1000 FPR < 1.5% (all 10 tested). Black-box robustness: 0/10 breached after 500 coordinate-ascent steps.

REFUTATION

Joint GCG defeats within-family canaries 55% of the time (n=20). Cross-lingual degradation 15-25pp. Monitorability law falsified (r=0.97 was encoder/decoder confound). Scan martingale value is calibration-free FAR, not superior detection.

RESIDUAL

Defense assumes gradient-based attacker. Template attacks are handled by primary classifier alone (99% hold). Adaptive attacker with cross-family joint optimization remains exponentially penalized but not impossible.

VERDICT

High confidence for bounded claim. Honest negative (monitorability) strengthens overall credibility. Geometric bound is the genuine theoretical contribution.

CRITICAL arXiv:2606.30566

Authority Is Not a Keyword

Preprint, 2025. Solo author. Under review (USENIX Security 2027).

CLAIM

Compliance to injected instructions is mechanistically orthogonal to safety refusal. Linear probes achieve AUC 1.0 separability. Multi-layer steering causally suppresses compliance 100% to 0%.

EVIDENCE

AUC 1.0 across 4 model families (Llama-70B, Qwen-32B, DeepSeek-32B, Gemma-27B). Compliance direction cosine with refusal direction: -0.025 (orthogonal). Cross-domain transfer: share/conceal AUC=1.0, allow/deny AUC=0.90. Perplexity uncorrelated (Spearman rho=-0.07). Behavioral analog confirmed on 6 frontier APIs.

REFUTATION

Probe trained on specific compliance domains may not generalize to novel instruction types. Causal intervention uses activation addition (linear assumption). Cross-domain AUC drops to 0.90 for allow/deny (not perfect). Only 4 open-weight model families tested mechanistically.

RESIDUAL

The orthogonality claim needs validation on larger proprietary models (only behavioral analog available). Steep sigmoid transition over ~1 alpha unit suggests fragility of the steering intervention at boundary conditions.

VERDICT

High confidence on separability. The orthogonality finding is the headline: safety training provides no protection against compliance attacks. Architectural intervention required.
